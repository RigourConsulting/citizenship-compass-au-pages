Sources and curriculum attribution

Last reviewed: October 2026

Official government source material and independent authoring

Citizenship Compass AU references official educational publications published by the Commonwealth of Australia. All 240 practice questions and pedagogical explanations are independently authored by Rigour Consulting.

Relationship between official materials and our practice content

It is important to understand the boundary between Australian Government publications and Citizenship Compass AU:

  • Official Australian Government source material. The Department of Home Affairs publishes the official testable public booklet, Australian Citizenship: Our Common Bond. This booklet defines the factual knowledge required for the citizenship test.
  • Citizenship Compass AU explanations. Rigour Consulting has structured the official text into 65 self-contained lessons across the four syllabus parts, providing plain-language summaries and concept comparisons.
  • Citizenship Compass AU practice content. All 240 practice questions across 120 item families are original educational drills authored by Rigour Consulting to evaluate your grasp of the official curriculum. They are not actual or past examination questions administered by the Department of Home Affairs.
  • Internal practice blueprint. Our curriculum blueprint maps each lesson to specific learning objectives, ensuring comprehensive coverage across Australia's democratic heritage, legal systems, and shared values.

Authoritative curriculum breakdown

Our 65 lessons correspond to the four testable sections of Australian Citizenship: Our Common Bond:

Part 1

Australia and its people

Ancient First Nations cultures, colonisation, the federation of Australian colonies in 1901, national symbols (the Australian National Flag, the Aboriginal Flag, the Torres Strait Islander Flag, Commonwealth Coat of Arms, golden wattle, and national anthem), and Australia's multicultural society.

Part 2

Democratic beliefs, rights and liberties

Key democratic principles: parliamentary democracy, rule of law, living peacefully, respect for all individuals, freedom of speech and expression, freedom of association, freedom of religion, equality of opportunity, and equality under the law.

Part 3

Government and the law in Australia

The Australian Constitution, the role of the Head of State (the King represented by the Governor-General), the Head of Government (the Prime Minister), the Federal Parliament (House of Representatives and Senate), the three tiers of government, passing legislation, and the independent judicial system.

Part 4

Australian values

The dedicated values curriculum introduced in November 2020: commitment to the rule of law, parliamentary democracy, equality of men and women, freedom of religion, mutual respect and tolerance, active community participation, and learning the English language as an unifying national element.
